How it works

Three beats, in order

  1. 01
    Agent drafts

    Your agent finishes the words itself: the email, the quote reply, the reminder. It doesn't send it. It shows you the exact text, not a summary of what it plans to do.

  2. 02
    You see and tap

    The draft lands on your phone, word for word. Approve it. Veto it, and veto always wins. Or let the hold clock run: it releases only once you've verifiably seen it.

  3. 03
    Receipt lands

    What was sent, who approved it, when. A permanent line in the ledger. Yours to point to, months later, without asking anyone to remember.

We ran the battery on ourselves first

Signet runs Syahi's own approvals. On August 31, 2026, we put it through a live battery against our own production releases, on the phone, with real clocks. Here's what happened, told straight.

  • Hash mutation, caught item B

    A draft's content changed after the founder had already looked at it. The old approval died on the spot; the system didn't let the changed text ride on a stale seal. It re-flagged the item as new and made her look again. She vetoed it. This is what content-pinning means in practice: you approve the exact bytes, not a label that says "email sent."

  • Veto won the race item C

    An item was already counting down on its hold clock, seconds from release. The veto beat the clock. It never went out, and the veto is on the record.

  • Freeze, from a phone item A

    One tap, from her phone, froze every pending release at once, mid-clock. No agent could lift it. Only she could authorize the lift. That's the rule now: any agent may freeze, no agent may unfreeze.

  • One send, only after her seal item E

    One real email went out: checked against the approved recipient and the signed template, sent only after she approved it, sealed in the record.

Two other drafts never made it to her phone at all. The system's own checks refused them first: one recipient wasn't on the approved list, one body had drifted off template. Caught before they became her problem, not after.

The seal can travel with the message

Every approved send already mints a signed certificate. Opt a draft class in, and the send carries it outward: one footer line whose link opens a public verification page. The recipient sees who approved this exact message and when, checked against the signature in their own browser. No account, no trust-us.

…so the three overdue invoices are attached again for convenience. Best regards, invoice-chaser

Reviewed and approved by you on 2026-09-03. Verify: approve.syahi.sh/s/cmd_9f2…

Off by default, opted in per draft class. And the guarantee runs the other way too: a rejected or unverified send can never carry the footer. It's appended only after the certificate verifies.
